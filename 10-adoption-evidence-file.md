## 1. The failure record (adoption of generic AI)

**E1. MIT, The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025 (MIT NANDA initiative, published mid 2025).**
Finding: 95 percent of enterprise generative AI pilots delivered no measurable P&L return; only about 5 percent reached production with measurable value, against an estimated 30 to 40 billion dollars of enterprise investment. Root cause named by the authors: the learning gap, tools that cannot retain feedback, adapt to context, or improve over time. Supporting findings worth knowing: externally built vendor tools reached production about twice as often as internal builds, and the study documents a shadow AI economy where roughly 90 percent of surveyed workers used personal AI tools regardless of official pilots.
Caveat for internal awareness: the study's sample and methodology drew criticism; treat 95 percent as the study's finding, always attributed, never as a settled universal fact.
Approved phrasing: "An MIT study of enterprise AI in 2025 found 95 percent of generative AI pilots returned nothing measurable. The authors' diagnosis was not model quality. It was that generic tools do not learn the operation they are dropped into." 
Use: adoption page (primary stat), decks, conversations.

**E2. S&P Global Market Intelligence enterprise survey, published March 2025 (over 1,000 respondents, North America and Europe).**
Finding: 42 percent of companies abandoned the majority of their AI initiatives before production, up from 17 percent the year before; the average organization scrapped 46 percent of proofs of concept; top cited obstacles were cost, data privacy, and security risk.
Approved phrasing: "S&P Global's 2025 survey found 42 percent of companies abandoned most of their AI initiatives before production, up from 17 percent a year earlier. The obstacles they named most: cost, data privacy, and security."
Use: adoption page (primary stat), homepage record strip, decks. The privacy and security obstacle finding is the single most Manar aligned sentence in the entire evidence base; use it whenever the sovereignty argument is being made.

**E3. Gartner prediction, July 2024.**
Finding: at least 30 percent of generative AI projects would be abandoned after proof of concept by end of 2025, citing poor data quality, inadequate risk controls, escalating costs, and unclear business value.
Approved phrasing: "Gartner predicted in 2024 that at least 30 percent of generative AI projects would be abandoned after proof of concept, naming poor data quality, weak risk controls, escalating costs, and unclear business value." Always the word predicted, never found; this is a forecast, not a measurement.
Use: adoption page failure causes section (the four causes structure the section), decks.

**E4. Gartner prediction, June 2025.** Over 40 percent of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027. Use: conversations and decks only when agents come up; not on the site (Manar does not sell against agents).

**E5. BCG, The Widening AI Value Gap, September 2025 (1,250 respondents, as reported).** About 60 percent of companies generate no material value from AI despite investment; roughly 5 percent create substantial value at scale. Verified via secondary reporting, not the primary document. Use: decks and conversation, attributed as "BCG reported in 2025"; keep off the site until primary verified.

**E6. McKinsey global AI survey, November 2025 (as reported).** 88 percent of organizations use AI in at least one function, yet only a minority report meaningful enterprise wide EBIT impact. Verified via secondary reporting. Use: conversation only, as the adoption paradox in one line: nearly everyone uses it, few can point to the money. Keep off the site until primary verified.

**E7. RAND Corporation, 2024.** AI project failure estimated at roughly 80 percent, about double traditional IT projects. Use: internal framing only; the estimate spans pre generative AI practice and invites methodology debate on a public page.

## 2. The commoditization record (generic AI losing its value)

**E8. Stanford HAI AI Index Report 2025.**
Finding: the inference cost of GPT-3.5 level performance fell from 20 dollars per million tokens in November 2022 to 0.07 dollars by October 2024, a 280 fold drop in about 18 months. Same report: 78 percent of organizations used AI in 2024, up from 55 percent in 2023; global corporate AI investment reached 252.3 billion dollars in 2024.
Approved phrasing: "Stanford's AI Index measured it: the cost of generic AI intelligence fell 280 fold in eighteen months. What everyone can rent for pennies is an advantage to no one. The advantage moved to what is trained on your conditions and owned on your ground."
Use: adoption page and homepage. This is the thesis the site has carried since launch, now with a measurement under it. It is the number that should sit closest to the existing premise copy.

## 3. The Pakistan record

**E9. National AI Policy 2025, approved by the federal cabinet on 30 July 2025** (Dawn and others reporting; policy document from the Ministry of IT and Telecom).
Facts safe to state: first national AI policy; built on six pillars (innovation ecosystem, awareness and readiness, secure and ethical AI, sectoral transformation, national infrastructure, international collaboration); headline targets include training one million AI professionals by 2030, an AI Innovation Fund and AI Venture Fund, 3,000 annual scholarships, 50,000 civic AI projects and 1,000 local AI products over five years; establishes an AI regulatory directorate and sectoral sandboxes.
Approved phrasing: "Pakistan's first National AI Policy, approved in July 2025, calls for local AI products, sectoral transformation, secure and ethical deployment, and one million trained professionals by 2030. Manar's work sits inside that direction: local products, deployed in Pakistani institutions, operated by Pakistani staff."
Use: adoption page (short section linking to the existing policy page on the site), the policy page itself, decks. Never claim endorsement, certification, participation, or funding. Alignment of direction only. The reported one billion dollar commitment announced at Indus AI Week in February 2026 may be referenced in conversation as reported news, not on the site.
Known gap, internal awareness: commentators note the data protection law remains pending, which strengthens rather than weakens the case for architectures where data never leaves the client, and that argument may be made without disparaging the policy.

## 4. Usage rules (binding, extends the doc 08 claims discipline)

1. Every number travels with its source and year, in copy and in speech. "95 percent of pilots fail" alone is forbidden; "an MIT study in 2025 found" is the form.
2. Maximum three statistics per page, per deck section, per conversation opening. The evidence file is deep so the surfaces can be restrained.
3. Predictions are labeled predictions (E3, E4). Measurements are labeled by their study. The two are never blended into one sentence.
4. Secondary verified items (E5, E6, E7) stay off the public site until verified against primary documents. Task for the marketing hire when onboarded.
5. No schadenfreude. The record is presented as diagnosis of an approach, never as ridicule of organizations that tried. They are the customers.
6. Never imply the failure record proves Manar succeeds. It proves the causes are known; Manar's proof remains its own measured deployments, per the standing rule that only the eval harness produces Manar numbers.
7. Currency: figures about a fast moving field decay. The six month re verification is not optional; expired figures come off surfaces until re checked.
